Domain 3 - Misclassified Comparison Bias Checklist (Structured for LLM Use)

Question ID: 3.1
Category: Conditional
When to answer: If study type is observational; otherwise select Not applicable
Question: Were intervention/exposure group and comparator group of interest sufficiently well-defined so no meaningful vagueness remains for intended assessment of causal effect of interest?
Notes/Examples: Select No or Seemingly no if intervention/exposure and comparator of interest are vaguely or poorly defined. Such definitions may be provided in a study protocol.
Allowed answers: Yes | Seemingly yes | Seemingly no | No | Unclear (No) | Not applicable

Question ID: 3.2
Category: Conditional
When to answer: If study type is observational; otherwise select Not applicable
Question: Were observed intervention/exposure group and comparator group appropriate for intended assessment of causal effect (i.e., causal effect of interest)?
Notes/Examples: Select No or Seemingly no when measure or classification of exposure/intervention is unlikely to be accurate or precise enough, for example when an imprecise or inaccurate biomarker is used as a measure of exposure.
Allowed answers: Yes | Seemingly yes | Seemingly no | No | Unclear (No) | Not applicable

Question ID: 3.3
Category: Conditional
When to answer: If study type is observational; otherwise select Not applicable
Question: Might measurement or classification of observed exposure, intervention, or comparator group have been incorrect due to error or influence of knowledge, experience, or desire?
Notes/Examples: Examples include intentional misclassification of exposure to yield desired outcome, and unintentional misclassification due to prior knowledge or cognitive bias.
Allowed answers: Yes | Seemingly yes | Seemingly no | No | Unclear (Yes) | Not applicable

Question ID: 3.4
Category: Optional
When to answer: Optional; assessors may skip if unfeasible. Select Not applicable if experimental treatments are applied.
Question: What are the predicted magnitude and direction of biases arising from misclassification or measurement of intervention, exposure, and/or comparator?
Notes/Examples: Quantitative assessment (e.g., simulation) may be conducted by risk-of-bias assessor(s) to predict magnitude and direction of bias for this study result.
Allowed answers: Intervention or exposure intolerably favored (*) | Intervention or exposure tolerably favored (**) | Comparator intolerably favored (*) | Comparator tolerably favored (**) | Intolerably towards no effect (*) | Tolerably towards no effect (**) | Intolerably away from no effect (*) | Tolerably away from no effect (**) | Unpredictable | Not applicable | Skip

Source note: Table B3 and (c) 2021 the authors.
Bias magnitude definitions: * Intolerable means the study result should not be considered valid enough in relation to predicted bias magnitude. ** Tolerable means the study result could be considered valid enough in relation to predicted bias magnitude.
