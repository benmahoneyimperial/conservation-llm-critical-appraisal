Domain 5 - Detection Bias Checklist (Structured for LLM Use)

Question ID: 5.1
Category: General (please answer)
When to answer: Always
Question: Was there any way for the outcome measure to be affected by knowledge of exposure, intervention, subjects or areas, or desire for a certain outcome?
Notes/Examples: E.g., select Yes or Seemingly yes if data collectors measuring outcomes or human subjects reporting outcomes were aware of study details.
Allowed answers: Yes | Seemingly yes | Seemingly no | No | Unclear (Yes)

Question ID: 5.2
Category: General (please answer)
When to answer: Always
Question: Was measured outcome appropriate for intended assessment of causal effect (i.e., causal effect of interest)?
Notes/Examples: E.g., select Yes or Seemingly yes if measured outcome is consistent with intended outcome; select No or Seemingly no if measured outcome differs from pre-specified outcome or is inappropriate for intended causal-effect assessment.
Allowed answers: Yes | Seemingly yes | Seemingly no | No | Unclear (No)

Question ID: 5.3
Category: General (please answer)
When to answer: Always
Question: Were methods for measuring outcome data the same across groups?
Notes/Examples: E.g., select No or Seemingly no if outcome was measured at different time windows between groups (e.g., 2 weeks after intervention vs 4 weeks after control treatment). When exactly the same methods cannot be used due to study nature, assessors may select Yes or Seemingly yes if methods are sufficiently comparable. Example: bird species diversity measured in fields where detectability differs slightly between groups but can still be considered comparable.
Allowed answers: Yes | Seemingly yes | Seemingly no | No | Unclear (No)

Question ID: 5.4
Category: Conditional
When to answer: If 5.3 is No, Seemingly no, or Unclear
Question: Were potential differences in measured outcomes between groups investigated and adjusted/corrected if necessary?
Notes/Examples: Differences in measured outcomes between groups can be a source of detection bias.
Allowed answers: Yes | Seemingly yes | Seemingly no | No | Unclear (No) | Not applicable

Question ID: 5.4 (Optional item; numbering follows source)
Category: Optional
When to answer: Optional; assessors may skip if unfeasible
Question: What are the predicted magnitude and direction of biases arising from systematic differences in outcome measurement?
Notes/Examples: Quantitative assessment (e.g., simulation) may be conducted by risk-of-bias assessor(s) to predict magnitude and direction of bias for this study result.
Allowed answers: Intervention or exposure intolerably favored (*) | Intervention or exposure tolerably favored (**) | Comparator intolerably favored (*) | Comparator tolerably favored (**) | Intolerably towards no effect (*) | Tolerably towards no effect (**) | Intolerably away from no effect (*) | Tolerably away from no effect (**) | Unpredictable | Skip

Source note: Table B5.
Bias magnitude definitions: * Intolerable means the study result should not be considered valid enough in relation to predicted bias magnitude. ** Tolerable means the study result could be considered valid enough in relation to predicted bias magnitude.
