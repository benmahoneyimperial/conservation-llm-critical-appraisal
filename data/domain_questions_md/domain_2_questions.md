Domain 2 - Post-Intervention/Exposure Selection Bias Checklist (Structured for LLM Use)

Question ID: 2.1
Category: General (please answer whichever suitable)
When to answer: Always
Question: Choose and answer one of the following subquestions.
Subquestion selector options: Answer 2.1.a | Answer 2.1.b
Subquestion 2.1.a: Was selection of subjects or areas after intervention or exposure random or systematic (i.e., based on random or systematic sampling), and could exchangeability between groups be assumed based on the selection approach?
Subquestion 2.1.b: Was the entire (or nearly entire) population of inference followed up after intervention or exposure, and could exchangeability between before and after groups be assumed?
Notes/Examples: 2.1.a applies when an attempt was not made to collect data of the entire, or nearly entire, population of inference. 2.1.b applies when an attempt was made to collect data of the entire, or nearly entire, population of inference. Exchangeability might be assumed if groups were exchangeable (comparable), and the hypothetical effect estimate under exchanged conditions (swap) would not also be affected by post-intervention/exposure selection bias.
Allowed answers: Yes | Seemingly yes | Seemingly no | No | Unclear (No)

Question ID: 2.2
Category: General (please answer)
When to answer: Always
Question: Was/were the researcher(s) unaware (or blinded) of the selection of subjects or areas?
Notes/Examples: Non-blinding of selection might influence selection after intervention/exposure.
Allowed answers: Yes | Seemingly yes | Seemingly no | No | Unclear (No)

Question ID: 2.3
Category: General (please answer)
When to answer: Always
Question: After start of intervention/exposure or during analysis, were any subjects or areas excluded or lost from the study or analysis?
Notes/Examples: Excluding some subjects, areas, or collected data might increase risk of post-intervention/exposure selection bias.
Allowed answers: Yes | Seemingly yes | Seemingly no | No | Unclear (Yes)

Question ID: 2.4
Category: Conditional
When to answer: If 2.1 is No, Seemingly no, or Unclear, or if 2.3 is Yes, Seemingly yes, or Unclear; otherwise select Not applicable
Question: Were subjects or areas included in the study (or analysis) comparable between groups, allowing valid comparison (i.e., exchangeability or conditional exchangeability between groups)?
Notes/Examples: Select No or Seemingly no when groups are not comparable. Example: for effects of air pollutants on plant growth, resistance to pollutants may differ. If only pollutant-resistant individuals are included in the exposure group while both less-resistant and pollutant-resistant individuals are included in control, the effect estimate may be biased. If only pollutant-resistant individuals are selected in both groups, conditional exchangeability may hold.
Allowed answers: Yes | Seemingly yes | Seemingly no | No | Unclear (No) | Not applicable

Question ID: 2.5
Category: Conditional
When to answer: If 2.4 is No, Seemingly no, or Unclear; otherwise select Not applicable
Question: Were differences between groups likely explained by intervention/exposure or by a variable influenced by intervention/exposure (including outcome)?
Notes/Examples: Some intervention- or exposure-influenced variables may be unmeasured. Such unmeasured variables are sometimes called latent variables.
Allowed answers: Yes | Seemingly yes | Seemingly no | No | Unclear (Yes) | Not applicable

Question ID: 2.6
Category: Conditional
When to answer: If 2.5 is Yes, Seemingly yes, or Unclear; otherwise select Not applicable
Question: Did the author(s) adjust for potential post-intervention/exposure selection bias appropriately?
Notes/Examples: E.g., stratification to pool stratum-specific outcomes.
Allowed answers: Yes | Seemingly yes | Seemingly no | No | Unclear (No) | Not applicable

Question ID: 2.7
Category: Optional
When to answer: Optional; assessors may skip if unfeasible
Question: What are the predicted magnitude and direction of biases arising from systematic differences in selection of subjects or areas into study or analysis after intervention or exposure?
Notes/Examples: Quantitative assessment (e.g., simulation) may be conducted by risk-of-bias assessor(s) to predict magnitude and direction of bias for this study result.
Allowed answers: Intervention or exposure intolerably favored (*) | Intervention or exposure tolerably favored (**) | Comparator intolerably favored (*) | Comparator tolerably favored (**) | Intolerably towards no effect (*) | Tolerably towards no effect (**) | Intolerably away from no effect (*) | Tolerably away from no effect (**) | Unpredictable | Skip

Source note: Table B2 and (c) 2021 the authors.
Bias magnitude definitions: * Intolerable means the study result should not be considered valid enough in relation to predicted bias magnitude. ** Tolerable means the study result could be considered valid enough in relation to predicted bias magnitude.
