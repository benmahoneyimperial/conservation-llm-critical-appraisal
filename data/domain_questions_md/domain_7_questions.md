Domain 7 - Outcome Assessment Bias Checklist (Structured for LLM Use)

Question ID: 7.1
Category: General (please answer)
When to answer: Always
Question: Was/were person(s) who estimated intervention effectiveness or exposure impact aware of exposure/intervention received by subjects or areas?
Notes/Examples: E.g., select Yes or Seemingly yes if analysts were aware of study details.
Allowed answers: Yes | Seemingly yes | Seemingly no | No | Unclear (Yes)

Question ID: 7.2
Category: General (please answer)
When to answer: Always
Question: Is it likely there is/are error(s) or inappropriate methods in applied descriptive statistical analyses?
Notes/Examples: E.g., miscalculations of sample sizes, means, medians, variances, ranges for intervention/exposure and comparator groups, or error in converting analogue to digital data.
Allowed answers: Yes | Seemingly yes | Seemingly no | No | Unclear (Yes)

Question ID: 7.3
Category: Conditional
When to answer: If inferential statistics are applied; otherwise select Not applicable
Question: Is it likely there is/are error(s) in applied inferential statistics (including null hypothesis testing, estimation, coding)?
Notes/Examples: E.g., miscalculations of differences between intervention/exposure and comparator, coding errors, etc.
Allowed answers: Yes | Seemingly yes | Seemingly no | No | Unclear (Yes) | Not applicable

Question ID: 7.4
Category: Conditional
When to answer: If inferential statistics are applied; otherwise select Not applicable
Question: Were assumptions for applied inferential statistics violated, or were applied inferential methods inappropriate for inferential goal(s)?
Notes/Examples: E.g., inappropriate sample sizes for hypothesis testing; normality not assumed when conducting a parametric test; equal or unequal variances not tested when testing for difference; no justification for dependent and independent variable choice; Pearson correlation used when analyzing a causal relationship; inappropriate comparison of multiple models where some models do not relate to impact/effectiveness; inappropriate modeling that may affect effectiveness/impact estimates.
Allowed answers: Yes | Seemingly yes | Seemingly no | No | Unclear (Yes) | Not applicable

Question ID: 7.5
Category: Optional
When to answer: Optional; assessors may skip if unfeasible
Question: What are the predicted magnitude and direction of biases due to errors in applied statistical methods?
Notes/Examples: Quantitative assessment (e.g., simulation) may be conducted by risk-of-bias assessor(s) to predict magnitude and direction of bias for this study result.
Allowed answers: Intervention or exposure intolerably favored (*) | Intervention or exposure tolerably favored (**) | Comparator intolerably favored (*) | Comparator tolerably favored (**) | Intolerably towards no effect (*) | Tolerably towards no effect (**) | Intolerably away from no effect (*) | Tolerably away from no effect (**) | Unpredictable | Skip

Source note: Table B7 and (c) 2021 the authors.
Bias magnitude definitions: * Intolerable means the study result should not be considered valid enough in relation to predicted bias magnitude. ** Tolerable means the study result could be considered valid enough in relation to predicted bias magnitude.
