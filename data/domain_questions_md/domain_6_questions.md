Domain 6 - Outcome Reporting Bias Checklist (Structured for LLM Use)

Question ID: 6.1
Category: General (please answer)
When to answer: Always
Question: Are reported relevant outcome data (or effect estimate) likely to be from selected measurements of the outcome?
Notes/Examples: I.e., only part of measured outcomes is reported. Example: only 80 measured outcomes are reported when there are 100, or the effect estimate is based on 80 measured outcomes when there are 100.
Allowed answers: Yes | Seemingly yes | Seemingly no | No | Unclear (Yes)

Question ID: 6.2
Category: General (please answer)
When to answer: Always
Question: Are relevant outcome data likely to be unreported for some subgroup(s)?
Notes/Examples: I.e., only outcome data on certain subjects or areas with certain characteristic(s) (e.g., taxonomic group), or in certain conditions (e.g., intervention intensity), are available.
Allowed answers: Yes | Seemingly yes | Seemingly no | No | Unclear (Yes)

Question ID: 6.3
Category: General (please answer)
When to answer: Always
Question: Is/are analysis/analyses of the causal relationship of interest (intervention-outcome or exposure-outcome) likely to be partially reported?
Notes/Examples: I.e., there is/are other relevant analysis/analyses of the causal relationship that is/are not reported.
Allowed answers: Yes | Seemingly yes | Seemingly no | No | Unclear (Yes)

Question ID: 6.4
Category: Optional
When to answer: Optional; assessors may skip if unfeasible
Question: What are the predicted magnitude and direction of biases in reporting of study findings?
Notes/Examples: Quantitative assessment (e.g., simulation) may be conducted by risk-of-bias assessor(s) to predict magnitude and direction of bias for this study result.
Allowed answers: Intervention or exposure intolerably favored (*) | Intervention or exposure tolerably favored (**) | Comparator intolerably favored (*) | Comparator tolerably favored (**) | Intolerably towards no effect (*) | Tolerably towards no effect (**) | Intolerably away from no effect (*) | Tolerably away from no effect (**) | Unpredictable | Skip

Source note: Table B6 and (c) 2021 the authors.
Bias magnitude definitions: * Intolerable means the study result should not be considered valid enough in relation to predicted bias magnitude. ** Tolerable means the study result could be considered valid enough in relation to predicted bias magnitude.
